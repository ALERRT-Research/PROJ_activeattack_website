About the data

An active attack occurs when an individual or individuals is actively killing or attempting to kill multiple unrelated people in a public space.

The key word is actively. The definition excludes events that law enforcement learns of only after they end, such as many family annihilations in a private home; first responders are typically alerted to an active attack while it is happening. Even so, more than half of these attacks end before law enforcement arrives, because civilians stop the attacker or the attacker stops (flees or dies by suicide). Any weapon counts, whether a firearm, a knife or a vehicle, as long as the intent is to kill as many people as possible.

ALERRT began collecting data on active shooter events in 2010 to understand how they unfold and to inform its training, and in 2013 joined the FBI to form an Active Shooter Working Group that supports the FBI’s annual reports. Around 2018 the definition widened from firearms to any weapon, and the data became the Active Attack Data: every FBI-documented firearm attack, plus the non-firearm attacks identified by ALERRT’s research team.

For how “active attack” differs from terms often used in the media, see Sandel & Martaindale (2022), What Are We Talking About? Definitional Confusion Within Active and Mass Shooting Research, Journal of Mass Violence Research.

More about the ALERRT Center’s research at ALERRTResearch.org and its training at ALERRT.org. Questions about the data: see Contact.
